LEARN · PRACTISE · CHOOSE

Practical support, shaped around real lives.

Every community has different strengths and needs. Our focus areas offer flexible starting points for conversations, shared learning and useful next steps—not a one-size-fits-all promise.

Ask about our approach
FOUR CONNECTED FOCUS AREAS

Start where a useful question leads.

Each area can stand on its own or connect with others, depending on what women and girls say would help.

01 · LEARNING

Education & everyday confidence

Learning can open access to information, choices and new connections. Activities may support continued learning, digital basics and the confidence to ask questions.

Possible starting points

  • Learning support and study habits
  • Digital and information literacy
  • Mentoring and guidance conversations

A learning activity should match participants' interests, language and available resources.

02 · PRACTICAL SKILLS

Skills that connect to her goals

Hands-on practice can help women explore what they enjoy, what they already know and which skills could be useful in their local context.

Possible starting points

  • Vocational and craft-based practice
  • Workplace and communication skills
  • Introductory digital and money skills

Training alone does not guarantee employment or income; options depend on local opportunities.

03 · WELLBEING & AWARENESS

Reliable information, shared respectfully

Small group conversations can make it easier to raise questions, understand healthy practices and learn where reliable local support may be available.

Possible starting points

  • Health, hygiene and wellbeing awareness
  • Nutrition and healthy daily practices
  • Information on appropriate local services

Awareness activities complement—not replace—qualified medical advice and care.

04 · LIVELIHOOD & ENTERPRISE

Explore options before taking a leap

For women interested in earning, a first step may be understanding costs, customers, time and support before deciding whether an idea fits.

Possible starting points

  • Basic business planning and budgeting
  • Peer exchange and local market questions
  • Finding trusted sources of guidance

Business outcomes depend on each person's circumstances and the wider market.

HOW A PROGRAM CAN TAKE SHAPE

Listen first, then plan together.

01

Hear local priorities

Talk with women and community partners about interests, barriers and existing knowledge.

02

Agree a useful first step

Choose an activity that suits the audience, setting, time and resources available.

03

Reflect and adapt

Ask what participants found useful and use their feedback to shape what happens next.
